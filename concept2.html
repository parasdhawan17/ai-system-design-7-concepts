Concept 2 of 7

Fine-Tuning — Teach the Model How to Behave

Fine-tuning changes the model itself with examples. Use it for behaviour, format, and style — not for facts that change often.

THE BIG IDEA

The goal: teach how to behave, not what to know

Fine-tuning trains the model again, on your examples. The model learns a pattern of behaviour.

It learns how to answer. It is a poor way to teach it new facts.

WHEN TO USE IT

Good reasons to fine-tune

  • Consistent format: Always reply in one exact structure, like JSON with fixed fields.
  • Style and tone: Always sound like your brand. Short, formal, friendly — one voice.
  • Classification and extraction: Label tickets. Pull names, dates, and amounts from text.
  • Domain behaviour: Act like a legal assistant, a medical coder, or a support agent for one product.

You need good training examples. Many teams start with hundreds to thousands of clean input–output pairs. Bad examples teach bad behaviour.

RAG vs FINE-TUNING

The comparison every interview asks

RAGFine-tuning
ChangesThe context at answer timeThe model itself
Best forFresh, private, changing knowledgeBehaviour, format, style, stable skills
Update costLow. Edit the document, re-index.High. Collect data, train, test, deploy again.
CitationsYes. Answers can cite sources.No natural citations.
Common use togetherMost real systems use both: fine-tune for behaviour, RAG for knowledge.
COST TRICK + MISTAKE

LoRA, PEFT, and the classic error

LoRA / PEFT means parameter-efficient fine-tuning. You train only a small added part, not the whole model. Training is much cheaper and faster. Results are often close to full fine-tuning.

Common mistakeDo not fine-tune a model just because it does not know information that changes often. The model will learn stale facts. Use RAG for that job.

Quick quiz

  1. Your model must answer with today’s prices. Fine-tuning or RAG?
Show answer

RAG. Prices change often. Fine-tuning would freeze old prices into the model. Fine-tune for behaviour; retrieve for fresh facts.
